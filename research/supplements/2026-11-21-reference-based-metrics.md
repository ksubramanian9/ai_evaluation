# Research supplement: reference-based-metrics

As of 2026-11-21. Web access worked; ACL Anthology PDFs downloaded and converted with pdftotext.

## Claims

- **BLEU definition (Papineni et al. 2002).** Modified n-gram precision: candidate counts clipped by the maximum count in any single reference (Countclip = min(Count, Max_Ref_Count)); computed over the corpus by summing clipped counts; geometric mean with N = 4 and uniform weights; multiplicative brevity penalty computed over the whole corpus, not per sentence; only case folding as normalization. Footnote: "BLEU only needs to match human judgment when averaged over a test corpus; scores on individual sentences will often vary from human judgments." More references raise scores: a human translator scored 0.3468 with four references and 0.2571 with two. System-level correlation with monolingual judges 0.99 and bilingual 0.96 over 5 systems. https://aclanthology.org/P02-1040/ (read: full text).
- **ROUGE (Lin 2004).** "Recall-Oriented Understudy for Gisting Evaluation". ROUGE-N is n-gram recall against references (BLEU is precision-based); ROUGE-L uses longest common subsequence, ROUGE-W weighted LCS, ROUGE-S skip-bigrams (ROUGE-SU adds unigrams). Multiple references: max pairwise score, with jackknifing in the package. Correlations are system-level (systems' average ROUGE vs average human coverage scores) on DUC 2001-2003. Conclusions: ROUGE-2, L, W, S worked well for single-document summaries; ROUGE-1, L, W, SU4, SU9 for very short summaries; high-90s correlation "was hard to achieve for multi-document summarization"; excluding stopwords usually helped; multiple references improved correlation. Few samples (~30 per system) made multi-document correlations unstable. https://aclanthology.org/W04-1013/ (read: full text).
- **Callison-Burch, Osborne, Koehn 2006.** Abstract: "an improved Bleu score is neither necessary nor sufficient for achieving an actual improvement in translation quality", with "two significant counterexamples". Because BLEU has no order constraint beyond n-grams and no synonym matching, an example hypothesis has at least 40,320 permutations with a similar score; some NIST 2005 hypotheses exceed 10^73. Content words and function words weigh equally. NIST 2005 Arabic-English: the entry ranked 1st by humans was 6th by BLEU (a human-aided, post-edited system); adequacy R^2 = 0.14 with it, 0.87 without. Systran (rule-based) was "vastly" underestimated vs SMT systems. Appropriate uses: tracking incremental changes to one system, comparing systems with similar strategies, parameter tuning. Inappropriate: comparing radically different systems, aspects BLEU does not model, rare improvements. Says Meteor and similar metrics may be affected too. https://aclanthology.org/E06-1032/ (read: full text).
- **chrF (Popović 2015).** Character n-gram F-score (6-gram); system-level correlations on WMT12-14 and segment-level on WMT14; chrF3 had the highest segment-level correlations for translation from English in WMT14; language- and tokenisation-independent. https://aclanthology.org/W15-3049/ (read: abstract and conclusions from the PDF).
- **BERTScore (Zhang et al.).** Token matching via contextual embeddings rather than exact match; evaluated on outputs of 363 MT and captioning systems; abstract states it "correlates better with human judgments" than existing metrics; adversarial paraphrase robustness. https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.09675 (read: abstract only).
